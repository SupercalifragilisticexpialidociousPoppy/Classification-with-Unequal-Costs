\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

We set out to build a spam filter that is judged by what its mistakes cost, not by how many it
makes. For every email the filter takes one of three actions: deliver it to the inbox, send it to the
spam folder, or hold it for a person to inspect. The two errors are very different. A spam in the
inbox is a small nuisance; a real email in the spam folder may never be seen. We priced them as
follows:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Action & real email ($y=0$) & spam ($y=1$) \\
\midrule
deliver       & $0$           & $\cfn=1$ \\
spam folder   & $\cfp=10$     & $0$ \\
inspect       & $\ci=0.3$     & $\ci=0.3$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If a model says an email is spam with probability $\post$, delivering it costs $\cfn\post$ on average,
filing it as spam costs $\cfp(1-\post)$, and inspecting it costs $\ci$. Choosing the cheapest of the
three~\cite[Section~1.5]{bishop2006} gives the rule
\[
  \text{deliver if } \post\le\thz=\frac{\ci}{\cfn}=0.3,\qquad
  \text{spam folder if } \post\ge\tho=1-\frac{\ci}{\cfp}=0.97,\qquad
  \text{inspect in between.}
\]
Without inspection the rule is a single cut at $\tstar=\cfp/(\cfp+\cfn)=10/11\approx0.91$, not the
$0.5$ that maximises accuracy, and inspection is worth offering only if it costs less than
$\cstar=\cfp\cfn/(\cfp+\cfn)=10/11$. Everything we report is the \emph{realized loss}: the average of
these costs over held-out emails.

Each section below is one member's part of the project, in the order the work built up. Member 6
set up the shared pipeline and an exact model that predicts what to expect
(Section~\ref{sec:m6}). Members 3 and 2 built the two classifiers, and Member 2 used them to compare
the accuracy rule with the cost rule (Sections~\ref{sec:m3} and~\ref{sec:m2}). Member 5 checked how
far the models' probabilities can be trusted and how to correct them when the share of spam changes
(Section~\ref{sec:m5}), and Member 1 tested that correction (Section~\ref{sec:m1}). Member 4 closed
the loop by measuring what inspection saves on held-out data (Section~\ref{sec:m4}). All models are
written from scratch in Python and NumPy.
