\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

We built a spam filter that is judged by what its mistakes cost, not by how many it makes. For every
email the filter takes one of three actions: deliver it to the inbox, send it to the spam folder, or
hold it for a person to inspect. The two errors are very different. A spam in the inbox is a small
nuisance. A real email in the spam folder may never be seen. We price them as follows:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Action & real email ($y=0$) & spam ($y=1$) \\
\midrule
deliver       & $0$           & $\cfn=1$ \\
spam folder   & $\cfp=10$     & $0$ \\
inspect       & $\ci=0.3$     & $\ci=0.3$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If a model says an email is spam with probability $\post$, delivering it costs $\cfn\post$ on average,
filing it as spam costs $\cfp(1-\post)$, and inspecting it costs $\ci$. The cheapest action is
\[
  \text{deliver if } \post\le\thz=\frac{\ci}{\cfn}=0.3,\qquad
  \text{spam folder if } \post\ge\tho=1-\frac{\ci}{\cfp}=0.97,\qquad
  \text{inspect in between.}
\]
Without inspection the rule is a single cut at $\tstar=\cfp/(\cfp+\cfn)=10/11\approx0.91$, not the
$0.5$ that maximises accuracy. Inspection is worth offering only if it costs less than
$\cstar=\cfp\cfn/(\cfp+\cfn)=10/11$. Everything we report is the \emph{realized loss}: the average
of these costs over held-out emails.

The rule is only as good as the probability $\post$ it is given, so most of the project is about
getting good probabilities and checking what happens when they are wrong. Each section below covers
the code one member wrote and what it showed. Member 6 built the shared pipeline that every
experiment uses (Section~\ref{sec:m6}). Members 2 and 3 wrote the two classifiers, logistic
regression and Gaussian models (Sections~\ref{sec:m2} and~\ref{sec:m3}). Member 5 wrote the
calibration tools and the correction for a changed spam rate (Section~\ref{sec:m5}). Member 1 tested
that correction (Section~\ref{sec:m1}), and Member 4 measured what inspection saves
(Section~\ref{sec:m4}). All models are written from scratch in Python and NumPy.
